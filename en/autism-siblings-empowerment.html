Personal empowerment for siblings of children on the autism spectrum: they deserve a place of their own too

In a family where one child is on the spectrum, the siblings need a place of their own too. On complex feelings, setting boundaries and building self-confidence and a personal sense of worth.

November 2, 2026 3-minute read By Alon Shemesh

In families where one of the children is on the autism spectrum, daily life may include adjustments, therapies, professional appointments and attention to unique needs. Alongside all this, brothers and sisters are growing up too, and sometimes from a young age they learn to be independent, considerate and to understand that their family works a little differently.

The relationship with a sibling on the autism spectrum can be a source of love, closeness and meaningful experiences. Still, complex feelings sometimes come up that aren't always easy to express.

Personal empowerment for the sibling of a child on the autism spectrum makes room for their own needs, strengthens their self-confidence and helps them develop a sense of worth that doesn't depend on their role within the family.

When a sibling becomes the child who always understands and gives in

In some families, the siblings get used to being the ones who understand the situation, stay flexible and try not to add to their parents' load.

Sometimes they give up family activities, avoid sharing their difficulties or feel they need to be more mature and responsible than other kids their age.

It isn't always an explicit expectation from the parents. Sometimes the child interprets the situation on their own and decides their needs are less urgent.

Over time, a feeling may develop that there isn't enough room for their personal wishes, their anger, their disappointments or even the simple need for attention.

It's okay to love your sibling and still feel frustrated

One of the emotional challenges that may come up for siblings of children on the spectrum is the difficulty of holding feelings that seem contradictory.

You can love your sibling, care about them and be happy about their successes, and at the same time feel jealousy, embarrassment, frustration or anger.

These feelings are natural and don't point to a lack of love or an unhealthy family bond.

When a child feels they aren't allowed to be angry or complain, they may hide their feelings and develop a sense of guilt.

Being able to talk freely about what they feel, without judgment, can help them understand that all their feelings deserve to be heard.

How do you strengthen self-confidence and a sense of worth?

Personal empowerment isn't only about coping with difficulties, but also about recognizing a person's strengths and abilities.

For siblings of children on the spectrum, the process may focus on a few significant areas:

  • Making room for personal needs: learning to recognize what I want and what matters to me, even when the family is dealing with additional needs.
  • Setting boundaries: understanding that it's okay to say no, to ask for privacy and not to take on responsibility that doesn't fit your age or personal ability.
  • Strengthening self-confidence: developing a sense of worth based on personal skills, wishes and successes.
  • Emotional expression: learning to share feelings of jealousy, anger, loneliness or frustration without being ashamed of them.
  • Developing independence and personal identity: building interests, friendships and goals that aren't necessarily tied to their role in the family.

Personal empowerment is also the chance to simply be me

Sometimes siblings of children on the spectrum get used to defining themselves through responsibility, consideration and caring for others.

A process of empowerment and personal growth can help them recognize thought and behavior patterns, strengthen their sense of capability and learn to make room for themselves too.

Siblings deserve emotional support too

Not every sibling of a child on the spectrum needs professional support. Many develop meaningful family bonds, independence and emotional resilience.

Still, when there is ongoing difficulty expressing feelings, low self-confidence, guilt or a tendency to regularly give up personal needs, it deserves attention.

At Nefeshlava - emotional therapy and personal coaching led by Alon Shemesh, you can work on strengthening self-confidence, identifying patterns that hold you back and developing tools for coping with emotional challenges, according to age and personal needs.

Because even in a family with a child who needs adjustments and support, every brother and sister deserves to feel seen, heard and given room to be who they are.
