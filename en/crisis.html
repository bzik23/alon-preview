Coping with crises and stress: finding stability in life again

There are times when it seems like everything is happening at once. Pressure at work, changes in the family, financial difficulties or major decisions that need to be made. Sometimes it's a sudden crisis that shakes your sense of security, and sometimes it's ongoing stress that builds up until it's hard to find a quiet moment.

Before we begin

We don't always know how to cope with what we're going through

Coping with crises and stress is an inseparable part of life, but we don't always know how to deal with what we're going through. When the thoughts won't stop, emotions flood in and your strength runs low, emotional support can help you understand the difficulty, strengthen your personal resources and find new ways of coping.

Emotional therapy with Alon Shemesh creates a personal, attentive and non-judgmental space where you can pause, understand what you're going through and gradually build a sense of stability and security.

An important distinction

What is the difference between stress and a personal crisis?

Stress is a natural response of body and mind to situations that are challenging, threatening or that require us to adapt quickly.

It can show up as a racing heart, muscle tension, restlessness or trouble concentrating. In some cases stress can help us stay alert and focused, but when it goes on for a long time or comes on very intensely, it can affect daily functioning and personal well-being.

A personal crisis, on the other hand, happens when someone goes through an event or a significant change that shakes their sense of stability, and they feel that the tools they had until now are not enough to deal with the situation. A crisis can follow job loss, divorce, the loss of someone close, illness or a major change in daily life.

The main difference is that stress can be part of everyday coping, while a crisis involves a feeling that reality has changed and that it's hard to adjust to it. Sometimes ongoing stress leads to a sense of crisis, and a personal crisis comes with significant emotional stress.
Signs worth knowing

When does stress become a difficulty that affects your life?

Stress is a natural response to challenging situations, and to some extent it even helps us focus and act. But when it goes on for a long time, it can affect daily functioning and quality of life. Here is how it may show up.

😣

Tension and inner restlessness

A feeling of tension, irritability or inner restlessness that stays with you throughout the day.

🔁

Thoughts that won't settle

Recurring thoughts and difficulty stopping the worry, even when you try to rest.

🔋

Fatigue and burnout

Emotional exhaustion and a lasting sense of burnout, even after rest.

🌙

Sleep, focus and decisions

Trouble sleeping, concentrating and making everyday decisions.

⚡

Sensitivity and anger

Heightened sensitivity, frustration or outbursts of anger that aren't like you.

🎒

More than you can handle

A feeling that what others demand of you is more than you can handle.

Sometimes a person keeps functioning normally on the outside, but inside feels like they're in a constant struggle. Stress therapy focuses not only on reducing tension, but also on identifying what increases it and developing tools for healthier coping.

Life crises

Coping with personal crises: when life changes direction

Not every crisis starts with a dramatic event. Sometimes it develops gradually, out of a feeling that something in life no longer fits. Personal crises can appear at different stages of life.

🧭

Midlife crisis

Questions about achievements, meaning, personal identity and the road still ahead.

💼

Job loss or a career change

A blow to your sense of security, financial uncertainty and difficulty finding a new direction.

🌅

Retirement

A change in daily routine, in the sense of being needed and in professional identity.

🕊️

Divorce or separation

Coping with the loss of the relationship, a change in the family structure and building a new routine.

🏡

Family changes

Moving home, children leaving home or a major change in life circumstances.

Despite the differences between these situations, a similar question often comes up: how do I go on from here? Coping with a crisis doesn't mean finding an immediate solution to every problem. Sometimes the first step is to understand what has changed, make room for the emotions that come up and identify which options are still in front of you.

How to cope

How do you cope with a crisis and regain a sense of control?

In times of stress or crisis, it's easy to feel that outside circumstances are running your life. But even when you can't change the situation itself, you can learn to influence the way you respond to it. An emotional therapy process may include several stages:

  1. Identifying the source of the difficulty: understanding the events, thoughts and feelings that create the sense of stress or crisis.
  2. Identifying patterns that hold you back: examining beliefs, thinking habits and automatic reactions that may intensify the difficulty.
  3. Developing tools for emotional regulation: practicing ways to calm down, reduce emotional overwhelm and cope with uncertainty.
  4. Strengthening personal resources: identifying abilities, past successes and sources of support that can help in the current period too.
  5. Building a way forward: defining practical steps, at your own pace, that help bring back a sense of capability and direction.

When the difficulty comes with an ongoing sense of difficulty making decisions or a loss of direction, working on strengthening your personal resources can gradually restore trust in your ability to choose and act.

Practical tools

Practical tools for coping with stress and crises

It isn't always possible to change the circumstances that cause stress or a crisis, but there are actions that can help lighten the emotional load and strengthen your sense of control.

🌬️

Mindful breathing and relaxation

Slow, comfortable breathing, meditation or relaxation help lower arousal and bring your attention back to the present moment.

🚶

Exercise and movement

Walking, swimming, yoga or any suitable movement help release tension, improve mood and give you a break from your thoughts.

😴

Sleep and keeping a routine

Regular sleep hours, regular meals and time to rest support your ability to cope during stressful periods.

🗂️

Managing load and priorities

Breaking big tasks into small steps, distinguishing between what is in your control and what isn't, and choosing what to focus on right now.

🤝

Sharing and accepting support

Talking with someone close or with a professional eases the sense of loneliness and offers another perspective on the situation.

💪

Building personal resilience

Recalling past challenges you've faced and identifying resources you already have help build a sense of capability.

How it works

Treating life crises and stress with NLP and guided imagery

With Alon Shemesh, the process combines listening, emotional conversation and tools from the world of NLP, alongside guided imagery and work with thinking patterns and limiting beliefs. The approach focuses on understanding how a person interprets reality, identifies their difficulties and responds to challenging situations.

Guided imagery makes it possible to practice relaxation, turn attention to inner sensations and imagine possible ways of coping. NLP tools are woven into the work on thinking patterns, inner dialogue and shifting your perspective on stressful situations. The process is tailored to each person's needs, without judgment and without any expectation to progress at a particular pace. Work on empowerment and personal growth is often part of the path to building resilience and regaining a sense of control.

In cases of severe emotional distress or a crisis that requires dedicated clinical treatment, it is important to turn to a licensed mental health professional.
When to reach out

You don't have to wait until stress becomes a crisis

Many people seek help only when they feel they have no strength left to cope, but you don't need to reach a breaking point to ask for support.

If you feel that stress is running your day, that you're struggling to adjust to a change or that you've lost your sense of direction, you can start exploring a different way. Sometimes simply having the chance to talk things through, understand what is really weighing on you and identify one small step you can take is a meaningful beginning.
