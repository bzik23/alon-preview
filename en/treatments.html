Anxiety that limits you, self-confidence that has worn down, stress that won't let go, or a relationship that hurts your sense of worth: focused emotional therapy helps you understand what lies behind these feelings, and develop new tools for facing life's challenges.

Before we begin

When something inside feels different

There are times in life when something inside feels different. Maybe you're dealing with anxiety and fears that limit you, experiencing low self-confidence, struggling to cope with ongoing stress, or in a relationship that hurts your sense of worth.

Sometimes the difficulty is clear and familiar, and sometimes it's hard to even explain what exactly is bothering you. You keep up your routine, work, manage relationships and function on the outside, but inside you feel it's time to change something.

Emotional therapy for adults lets you pause, understand what lies behind the feelings and behaviors that accompany you, and develop new tools for facing life's challenges.

At the Nefeshlava clinic, led by Alon Shemesh, therapy combines tools from the world of NLP, work on patterns of thinking and behavior, and personal guidance tailored to your needs and goals.

When to reach out

When is it worth seeking emotional therapy?

You don't have to go through a major crisis to ask for help. Sometimes it's precisely the small difficulties that build up over time that affect your quality of life, your relationships and how you feel.

You may recognize one or more of the following in yourself:

  • Anxiety, fears or phobias that lead you to avoid certain situations.
  • Low self-confidence, excessive self-criticism or a feeling that you're not good enough.
  • Emotional overload, ongoing tension and difficulty calming down.
  • Life crises following a breakup, a layoff, family changes or periods of transition.
  • Difficulty setting boundaries and protecting your own needs.
  • A toxic relationship involving control, criticism or emotional harm.
  • Patterns of thinking and behavior that keep repeating and make it hard for you to move forward.

Emotional therapy isn't only for dealing with a specific difficulty. It can also help you get to know yourself more deeply, strengthen your sense of capability and develop new ways of coping.

How it works

How does emotional therapy for adults with NLP work?

NLP is an approach that focuses on the connection between thoughts, emotions, language and patterns of behavior. With practical tools, you can examine how experiences and beliefs affect your reactions in the present, and practice new ways of coping.

Depending on the need, the process may combine several tools:

  • Identifying and changing thought patterns - examining beliefs and habits that limit how you function day to day.
  • Guided imagery - using the imagination to practice new responses and strengthen personal resources.
  • Emotional anchoring - practicing techniques that help you access feelings of calm and confidence.
  • Reframing - looking at situations from additional points of view to widen your range of responses.
  • Strengthening personal resources - identifying abilities, successes and strengths you can draw on when facing challenges.

Therapy doesn't follow a uniform template. Every person has their own pace, and the tools are chosen according to the goals of the process and the kind of difficulty.

Important to know: NLP is not a substitute for medical treatment or evidence-based treatments when they are needed, particularly in complex mental health conditions.
