Why am I always drawn to people who aren't right for me? 5 patterns worth knowing

Another relationship that began with excitement and ended in disappointment? 5 common patterns that draw us again and again to people who aren't right for us, and how to start changing them.

October 11, 2026 4-minute read By Alon Shemesh

This article is written for women and men alike.

Another first meeting that started with excitement, another relationship that looked promising, and once again the same disappointment. At first everything feels right: there's attraction, good conversations and the hope of something meaningful. But as the relationship moves forward, it turns out the other person isn't really available for a relationship, isn't looking for anything serious or simply isn't right for you.

If you keep finding yourself in the same situation, it may be worth asking a different question: not only why I meet people who aren't right for me, but what makes me choose them time after time?

Sometimes the answer has to do with patterns, beliefs and past experiences that shape our choices, even without our being aware of it.

5 patterns that repeat in choosing a partner

1. Being drawn to people who aren't truly available for a relationship

There's something intriguing about someone who's hard to get. Sometimes their unavailability makes us want to try harder and prove we're worthy of love. But a relationship where only one side invests can lead to ongoing frustration.

2. Confusing strong attraction with real compatibility

Butterflies and excitement are part of getting to know someone new, but they don't necessarily mean you're a good match. It's also important to check whether you share values, communicate well and both want the relationship.

3. Settling out of fear of being alone

Sometimes the wish for a relationship is so strong that we ignore things that bother us right from the start. Instead of asking whether the relationship is right for us, we're busy asking how to make it work.

4. Believing that with enough love, the other person will change

"Once we know each other better, he'll open up", "Later on she'll want something serious". The hope for change can keep us in a relationship that doesn't meet our needs. It's worth looking at the person as they are today, not only at the potential we see in them.

5. Going back to what's familiar, even when it isn't good for us

Sometimes familiar relationship patterns feel natural, even if in the past they led to disappointment. Getting to know these patterns can help us understand why we keep being drawn to the same kinds of relationships.

How can you tell attraction from real compatibility?

Before entering a new relationship, it's worth pausing to ask yourself three questions:

  • Do I feel comfortable being who I am around this person?
  • Do we both want similar things from the relationship?
  • Am I choosing this relationship out of real desire, or out of fear of being alone?

The answers aren't always simple, but they can help you spot gaps early on.

How do you start changing the pattern?

The first step is to identify what keeps repeating. Try thinking about past relationships: what attracted you at first? Which signs did you choose to ignore? And what would you like to do differently next time?

It isn't always easy to recognize these patterns on your own. Through coaching for finding a partner, you can explore beliefs, fears and recurring choices, and develop tools to approach new relationships with awareness and confidence.
