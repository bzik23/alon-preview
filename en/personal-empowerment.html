Personal empowerment - stop living on autopilot and start choosing

You don't need a big crisis to realize something needs to change. A process of empowerment and personal growth for people who feel there is more in them, and finally want to let it out.

What is personal empowerment When to start What we work on How it works Frequently Asked Questions
Before we begin

From the outside everything looks fine. Inside, you know it isn't quite right

There's a job, a home, a routine that ticks along. No one around you thinks there's a problem, and maybe you can't really point to one thing that's missing either. And still there's a quiet feeling that won't go away.

Maybe you know exactly what you'd like to change, and you've been putting off the first step for a long time. Maybe you give to everyone - at work, at home, to the kids - and at the end of the day there's hardly any room left for yourself. And maybe you wake up in the morning feeling that life is happening to you, instead of you leading it.

Most people who come to this process are not people in crisis. They are fully functioning people who have reached a point where they ask themselves one simple question: Is this the life I wanted for myself?

Personal empowerment starts right there. The moment you stop taking the current situation for granted, and start looking at what really matters to you, what is stopping you, and how to create change that fits the person you are today.

No slogans

What personal empowerment is - and what it isn't

Personal empowerment is an inner process in which a person takes back the wheel: recognizing their strengths, building a real sense of capability, learning to set boundaries and taking responsibility for the direction their life is heading.

In practice, the process rests on four principles:

  • Knowing your strengths - recognizing what you're truly good at, instead of measuring yourself only by what's missing.
  • A sense of capability - replacing "I'm not sure I can" with a well-founded belief that you can.
  • Setting boundaries - speaking up, asking and saying no without paying for it in guilt.
  • Taking control - stop reacting to circumstances, and start choosing them.

And it's just as important to say what the process is not. Personal empowerment isn't about becoming someone else, and it isn't a collection of motivational quotes that lift you for two days and disappear. It isn't positive thinking that covers up what hurts, and it isn't a course that ends with a certificate and a binder left on the shelf. It is a personal, practical process, measured by one thing only: what has changed in the way you live day to day.

And if you're wondering about the difference between the terms - personal growth is the movement, and personal empowerment is the force that drives it. In other words, personal growth is the path to personal empowerment: every small step you manage to take differently gives you back a little more trust in yourself, and that trust is what makes the next step possible. In practice both happen in the same process, so there's little point in separating them.

The real question

Why is it so hard to change things we already know need changing?

We can know we need to set a boundary - and not set it. Know it's time to decide - and put it off again. Know a certain habit is hurting us - and go back to it tomorrow morning.

This happens because change doesn't happen on the level of logic alone. Behind the way we act are habits etched in over years, beliefs we absorbed from home and our surroundings, fear of failure and sometimes, surprisingly, fear of success, and a need for approval that dictates decisions without us noticing. Some of these patterns are so familiar to us that we no longer recognize them as a choice at all.

People don't stay in place because they don't want to move. They stay because something else is holding them there - and usually they don't know what.

That's why in the process we don't settle for the question "what do you want to change". The more important question is what makes you keep doing exactly what you're doing today. That is the real starting point, and that's where tools from the world of NLP come in, tools that can identify the limiting belief and take it apart.

A quiet caveat

"But I should be able to do this on my own"

This is one of the most common thoughts among people who come to this process, and it's completely understandable. You've read, you've thought, maybe you've even understood what the problem is. So why isn't it changing?

Because knowing what needs to be done, and managing to do it over time, are two completely different things. When we are inside our own patterns, it's almost impossible to see them from the outside. That isn't failure, it's simply the angle of view.

And notice something interesting: when it comes to our children, we look for the best teacher. When something happens to the car, we take it to a professional without a second thought. But when the difficulty touches our own lives - a choice, our confidence, feeling stuck - we expect ourselves to manage alone, and usually turn to friends rather than to someone whose profession this is.

Personal coaching doesn't replace your own abilities. It creates a space to pause the routine, look closely at what's happening, and above all turn understanding into action in real life.

For women in the process

Women's empowerment - connecting to your strengths and choosing yourself

Women's empowerment is a process of personal growth that allows women to strengthen their self-confidence, recognize their abilities and strengths and create change in the areas of life that matter to them. Many women juggle career, relationship, family and social expectations, and sometimes find it hard to make room for their own wishes and needs. An empowerment process for women helps identify limiting thought patterns, cope with self-criticism, learn to set boundaries and develop a sense of worth that doesn't depend on approval from those around them.

Through emotional and personal guidance that combines tools from the world of NLP, it's possible to work on strengthening a sense of capability, making decisions from a place of confidence and changing patterns that hold back progress. The goal isn't to become someone else, but to connect to strengths that already exist and learn to express them in everyday life.

For men in the process

Men's empowerment - building confidence, resilience and connection to yourself

Men's empowerment is a process of personal and emotional growth that helps men know themselves deeply, strengthen their self-confidence and find a balance between social expectations and their own wishes and needs. Many men feel pressure to succeed, to show control and to be strong in every situation, even when they are dealing with difficulties in their relationship, at work or in their personal lives. A men's empowerment process makes it possible to re-examine limiting ideas about masculinity, develop the ability to express emotions, set boundaries and build healthier, more satisfying relationships.

In personal coaching that combines tools from the world of NLP, it's possible to identify behavior patterns that hold back progress, work on strengthening self-worth and develop assertive communication and confidence in making decisions. The goal is to allow every man to define for himself what masculinity means to him, to act from choice rather than outside pressure, and to create a life that fits his values and aspirations.

A word on terms

Personal development coaching or emotional therapy?

This question comes up in almost every intro call, and rightly so - the two terms get mixed up in the market and it isn't always clear what you're getting.

Personal development coaching mostly looks ahead: you define a goal, build an action plan and move toward it. Emotional therapy for personal empowerment also goes into what lies beneath the surface: the experiences, beliefs and fears that created the pattern in the first place.

With Alon Shemesh, these aren't two separate tracks. He is both an emotional therapist for personal empowerment and a personal development coach, a graduate of the Tut Tikshoret school and a certified NLP Master, and the process moves between the two approaches according to what each session calls for: sometimes you need a practical tool for the coming week, and sometimes you need to step back and understand where the difficulty came from in the first place.

What the two approaches share is that the responsibility stays with you. No coach and no therapist will change your life for you - they can help you see what's holding you back and give you tools to move. If you'd like to see the wider range of areas, you can go to the Personal Coaching page or get to know my path on the About page.
