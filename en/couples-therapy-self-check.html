Check yourselves: does your relationship crisis call for couples therapy?

Arguments have become routine and you're no longer sure whether it's a passing crisis or a sign the relationship needs help. 8 short questions to help you pause and assess the state of your relationship, without pressure and without judgment.

October 11, 2026 4-minute read By Alon Shemesh

Have arguments become part of your routine? Do you feel like you live in the same house but aren't really together anymore? Maybe the thought of separation or divorce has even come up lately, but you're still not sure it's what you really want.

Almost every couple goes through periods of difficulty, distance and disagreement. But how do you know when it's a passing crisis and when the relationship needs professional help?

Not every relationship crisis has to end in separation. Sometimes couples therapy for partners can help you understand what went wrong and explore whether the relationship can be rebuilt.

Before making major decisions about your shared future, it's worth pausing to assess the state of the relationship.

Check yourselves: 8 questions about the state of your relationship

Read the questions and answer them honestly for yourselves. There are no right or wrong answers here, just a chance to listen to what you really feel.

  • Do you fight over the same issues again and again? Even after a long conversation, you come back to the same arguments without reaching a solution.
  • Do you feel lonely within the relationship? You share a home and a routine, but you're missing closeness, sharing and the feeling that someone really understands you.
  • Have you stopped talking about what bothers you? You'd rather stay silent to avoid another fight, even when things are weighing on you.
  • Have intimacy and closeness between you decreased significantly? Hugs, touch, personal conversations and shared moments have become rare.
  • Do you feel that every conversation turns into accusations? Instead of listening to each other, you're busy defending yourselves, criticizing or proving who's right.
  • Is the tension between you affecting the atmosphere at home? You feel the children or other family members are affected by the tension, the silences or the fights.
  • Is the thought of separation or divorce coming up more and more? One or both of you are already considering living apart, even if no decision has been made yet.
  • Do you still want to improve the relationship, but don't know how? There's a desire to change things, but your attempts aren't bringing improvement.

So what do your answers say?

If you answered yes to several of the questions, you may be dealing with a relationship crisis that deserves attention.

No number of answers defines whether your relationship is in danger, and this questionnaire is not a diagnostic tool. Even one significant difficulty may justify asking for help.

It's important to remember that a relationship crisis doesn't necessarily lead to divorce. Couples therapy can help you understand the source of the difficulties, improve communication and explore options for change.

Between a relationship crisis and reconciliation: can the relationship be rebuilt?

When a relationship is in deep crisis, it can be hard to see past the anger, the disappointment and the sense of distance.

For couples who want to explore the possibility of reconciliation, it's important to create a space where the difficulties can be talked about, and to understand whether there's a shared wish to rebuild the relationship.

Couples therapy before divorce lets partners pause, examine the relationship with the help of a professional and find out whether it can be rebuilt. Even when separation is the right option, the process can help you reach a more considered and conscious decision.

You don't have to wait until the relationship reaches the edge

If you recognized yourselves in the questions, this may be the time to give your relationship some attention. There's no need to wait until the crisis becomes unbearable to ask for guidance.

I guide couples through difficulties and crises in their relationship, with attentive listening, without judgment and without blame. If you're unsure about continuing your shared path, you can reach out and explore together, with no commitment, what the right next step is for you.

You don't need to know in advance what you want to do. You can simply start with a conversation, and from there see together what suits you.
