Decision Making: How to Stop Deliberating and Start Choosing

Some decisions drag on for weeks, and others we make and instantly regret. Decision-making coaching helps you understand what is really holding you back, and build a way of choosing you can rely on.

When to reach out Why it's so hard Who decides about your life The tools in the process Frequently Asked Questions
Before we begin

Not making a decision is also a decision

There's one decision that has been following you around for a while. It comes up on the way to work, before you fall asleep, every time someone asks "So, what did you decide?" You weigh it, go back and forth, put it off, and end up back where you started.

Putting off a decision feels safer than making the wrong choice, but it comes at a price. Endless deliberation drains your energy, gives you no rest, and slowly wears down your confidence that you can trust yourself at all. And in the meantime, when we don't choose, circumstances or someone else usually choose for us.

Most people who come to this process are not people who don't know how to think. They are smart, capable people who, precisely because of that, see every side, every risk and every option, and that's exactly where they get stuck.

Decision-making coaching doesn't hand you the right answer from the outside. It helps you reach it yourself, with clarity about what matters to you and what is really holding you back, and with the confidence to stand behind your choice.

A good decision isn't one you can't get wrong. It's one where you know why you chose it.

The real question

Why is it so hard to decide, even when you know what makes sense?

If decision making were only a matter of logic, we would draw up a list of pros and cons and be done. But we all know it doesn't work that way. The decision gets stuck because entirely different forces are at work beneath the surface.

These are the things that usually hold a decision back:

  • Too many options - when there are too many paths, the mind doesn't settle, it shuts down. Every choice feels like giving up on all the rest.
  • Fear of making a mistake - the bigger the decision, the bigger the fear of regret, and sometimes it paralyzes more than any real option.
  • Wanting vs. pleasing - it's hard to decide when it isn't clear whether we are choosing what's right for us, or what won't disappoint our parents, our partner or the people around us.
  • Perfectionism - the search for the perfect choice, the one with no risk at all, pushes aside any decision that is merely good.
  • Beliefs from the past - an inner voice saying "you always get it wrong" or "I mustn't disappoint" dictates decisions without us noticing.

There is also an important difference between a hard decision and a complex decision. A complex decision involves a lot of information that needs to be organized and weighed, and it is solved with a method. A hard decision is one where the facts are clear, but something emotional won't let you choose. Most people who come to the process aren't stuck because of a lack of information, but because of what that information stirs up in them. So in the process we don't settle for asking "which is better"; we first ask what keeps you from deciding.

A perspective that changes everything

Who really decides about your life?

In psychology this is called locus of control, and it's one of the most important questions when it comes to decision making. It looks at how much you feel your life depends on you, as opposed to circumstances, luck and other people.

A person with an external locus of control feels that things simply happen to them. The big decisions are dictated by the situation, by other people's expectations or by however things turned out, so choosing is hard for them too, because deep down they don't really believe their choice carries weight. A person with an internal locus of control knows there is a connection between how they act and the life they live. They don't control everything that happens, but they are responsible for their response and their direction.

When we feel that life is happening to us, every decision is frightening. When we feel that we are leading it, a decision becomes a tool rather than a threat.

A central part of the process is shifting the locus of control inward. Not to load you with blame for everything that has happened, but to give you back the sense that the steering wheel of your life is in your hands, and that you are allowed to choose.

How we work

The tools that turn deliberation into a choice

No single formula fits every decision, but there is an organized way to work. These are the main tools used in the process, adapted to what you bring.

⚖️

Clarity about values

Before weighing options, we look at what truly matters to you. Once the values guiding you are clear, many decisions that seemed complicated become clearer, because you have a compass to check every alternative against.

🗺️

Mapping all the options

Tunnel vision that sees only two paths is a common trap. We lay all the options out on the table, even ones that seem unrealistic, and weigh each one against its benefits, its costs and how well it fits you.

🧠

Emotion vs. logic

A good decision isn't born from a spreadsheet alone, nor from a gut feeling alone. We learn to separate the two, listen to each one on its own, and then bring them together into a whole choice you can rely on.

🕊️

The fear behind the decision

Often what's stuck isn't the decision but the fear of getting it wrong. We identify the fear, examine what would really happen in the worst-case scenario, and turn it from a paralyzing obstacle into a bridge you can cross.

🧭

Trained intuition

Intuition is a powerful tool, but it is most accurate when you are calm and connected. We learn to tell a genuine inner voice apart from fear or pressure disguised as one, and to trust yourself without dismissing logic.

🃏

Cards and guided imagery

Sometimes the answer sits in a place that's hard to reach with words. Inspiration cards and guided imagery, central tools in the work, bypass the noise in your head and let you see the choice from a new angle.

The result in practice

Where you are today, and where you'd like to be

Sometimes the simplest way to understand what you're looking for is to see the gap between the sentence you come into the process with and the sentence you want to leave it with.

"I go along with whatever others decide for me"

→

I take the wheel and choose the direction

Change doesn't happen in a day. It happens when you start recognizing what holds you back in real time, and practice a different way of choosing, again and again, until it becomes natural.

How it works

What a decision-making coaching process looks like

1

Understand

Intro call and mapping: what decision is on the table, what you've already tried, and what difficulty or fear keeps recurring beneath the surface.

2

Decode

We look at what is really holding you back: a limiting belief, fear of making a mistake or a need to please. We connect you to the values that point to the choice that's right for you.

3

Explore

We lay out all the options, separate emotion from logic and examine each alternative, using tools from the world of NLP, cards and guided imagery.

4

Choose and act

You come away with a clear decision and a practical first step, along with a way to make the next decisions calmly and confidently too.

A quiet caveat

"I'm a grown adult, I should be able to decide on my own"

It's a very common thought, and a very reasonable one. You've already asked for advice, read up, made lists. So why is it still stuck?

Because when we are inside the dilemma, it's almost impossible to see it from the outside. When the question is stressful and we are alone with it, we tend to cut corners, avoid the painful answer, and return to the same inner conversation in different versions. It isn't a lack of ability, it's simply the angle of view.

And notice something interesting: when we need to make a big professional decision at work, we consult an expert without a second thought. But when the decision concerns our own life, we expect ourselves to manage alone, and usually turn to friends rather than to someone for whom this is their profession.

Personal coaching doesn't decide for you. It creates a place to pause, ask the right questions, and turn endless deliberation into a choice you stand behind.

A closing thought

There's no perfect choice, only a choice you stand behind

Many times we wait for the moment when we'll know for certain what the right thing is. But complete certainty almost never arrives before the decision, and sometimes it is actually created after it, along the way.

The goal of the process is not to guarantee you'll never make a mistake. It's to help you choose with clarity about what matters to you, and to develop the confidence that even if something doesn't go as planned, you have the tools to cope and choose again.

If there's a decision that has been dragging on for a while, you can start with a single conversation, and understand together what is really holding you back and how to get unstuck.

The first step is one you take on your own. The next decision you no longer have to make alone.
