Couples Therapy Before the Wedding: Starting Your Life Together on the Right Foot

Deciding to get married is one of the most exciting moments in life: you're in love, planning a shared future and dreaming of the home you'll build together. Alongside the excitement, questions sometimes come up that aren't always easy to talk about. Couples therapy before the wedding lets you dedicate time to what matters more than any of the event preparations: your relationship.

Just before the wedding

Why is it worth starting couples therapy even before the wedding?

At the start of a relationship, when infatuation is at its peak, it's easy to focus on what you have in common. Over time the differences begin to show as well: different habits, expectations that were never said out loud, different approaches to money, family or sharing responsibilities at home.

These gaps don't necessarily point to a problem in the relationship. Each partner comes with a life story of their own, with values, habits and communication patterns built up over the years. The challenge begins when the differences become a constant source of fights, frustration or a feeling that one partner doesn't understand the other.

Couples therapy for young couples makes it possible to identify these gaps early, talk about them openly and explore how to deal with them together. You don't have to come to therapy because of a crisis; even couples who feel good in their relationship can benefit from setting aside time for meaningful conversations and aligning expectations ahead of their life together.

The goal isn't to reach complete agreement on every topic, but to learn how to talk about the things where there are differences too, without hurting the sense of partnership.
What to talk about before the wedding

What is important to talk about before getting married?

Some topics don't always come up in everyday conversation, yet they can become very significant after the wedding. In therapy for pre-marriage couples, you can give them room in an open and calm conversation.

💰

Money and finances

How you manage shared expenses, and how each of you relates to saving, spending and financial commitments.

👶

Children and parenthood

Whether and when you want to have children, and which values and views on raising them matter to each of you.

👨‍👩‍👧

Extended family

How you deal with your parents' expectations, holidays and family members' involvement in your decisions as a couple.

⚖️

Career and sharing responsibilities

How you balance work, personal growth, running the home and time together.

💗

Closeness and intimacy

What makes each of you feel loved, wanted and appreciated.

🗣️

Handling disagreements

How you respond to anger, disappointment or disagreement, and how you get back to talking after a fight.

The goal isn't to agree on everything, but to learn to talk about what's different between you too, without hurting the sense of partnership. A short intro call comes with no commitment at all.

The repeating pattern

Help with Relationship Difficulties: Why Do the Same Fights Keep Coming Back?

Maybe you know the feeling: once again you argued about the same issue, once again one of you got hurt and the other feels misunderstood.

Sometimes a fight starts over something small, like being late, an unanswered message or a decision about the weekend, but behind it lie deeper needs and feelings. One partner experiences the situation as a lack of consideration, while the other feels restricted or criticized. When each of you reacts from your own interpretation, a cycle of reactions forms that feeds itself.

Working on relationship difficulties lets you examine these communication patterns, understand what triggers them and practice other ways of expressing feelings and needs.

Instead of focusing on who is right, you can start asking what each of you is trying to say, and what it would take for both of you to feel heard.
Tools from the world of NLP

Pre-Wedding Couples Therapy with an NLP Approach

The NLP approach deals with the connection between thoughts, interpretations, language and behavior patterns. In a relationship, we often react not only to what our partner says, but also to the meaning we give their words.

A sentence said out of tiredness

→

"Maybe he's just tired" instead of "this is rejection" - examining the automatic interpretation before it sparks hurt feelings or a sense of being criticized

Using tools from the world of NLP, you can work on recognizing thought patterns and automatic reactions, consider other interpretations and practice more mindful communication. In the guidance process with Alon Shemesh, you can focus on a few main areas:

  • Improving listening and understanding of your partner's point of view.
  • Expressing feelings and needs clearly and respectfully.
  • Recognizing situations that trigger tension and automatic reactions.
  • Developing practical tools for handling differences of opinion.

The work isn't meant to change either partner's personality, but to help each of you get to know yourself better and the way you act within the relationship.

Unsure?

Unsure whether to get married? That's also a reason to come and talk

Not every couple who starts a pre-marriage process has already set a wedding date.

Some couples have been together for a long time and love each other, but find it hard to decide about the next step. Sometimes the worry is about commitment, sometimes about significant gaps between the partners, and sometimes about pressure coming from family or the people around them. Questions like "Is this the right relationship for me?", "Will we manage to cope with the difficulties?" or "Am I ready to give up some of my independence?" are questions that deserve room.

Couples guidance before marriage isn't meant to persuade partners to marry or to separate. The goal is to allow an honest conversation, understand where the uncertainty comes from and explore the options with greater awareness and clarity.
How it works

How does therapy for pre-marriage couples work?

Every couple comes with different needs, so the guidance process is tailored to the topics on your mind.

1

Getting to know the relationship

We begin by getting to know both of you, the story of your relationship and the reasons you chose to reach out now in particular.

2

Defining what matters

Together we define the topics you want to work on and identify gaps in expectations ahead of your life together.

3

Explore and practice

We examine communication patterns and practice tools for handling everyday situations, so the change shows up at home too.

4

Moving at your pace

Sometimes we focus on one topic, such as communication or family involvement, and sometimes on broader preparation for your life together.

The length of the process varies according to the couple's goals, the complexity of the difficulties and your pace of progress.
